What this project does

Predicts binary order delay risk (late / on-time) across 180,000 supply chain records using three ML models — comparing not only accuracy but how well each model explains the drivers of delay. Key engineered features include order processing time, geocoded destination coordinates, and temporal decomposition of shipping dates.

Why interpretability matters here

A prediction alone is not enough for supply chain operations. Managers need to know why an order is at risk — to reroute, escalate, or adjust lead time. This study demonstrates that EBM matches black-box accuracy while providing direct, auditable explanations without SHAP as a post-hoc layer.

⚡

Expedited Orders Are the Core Risk

Same-day shipments with near-zero processing time are the highest-risk and hardest-to-predict segment. All three models fail here. Enhanced monitoring and pre-emptive buffer protocols should target this shipping class specifically.

🌏

Pacific Asia Needs Separate Treatment

Errors concentrate disproportionately in Pacific Asia — not because of small sample size (Africa is smaller), but due to structural feature differences. A region-specific model or calibrated risk adjustment would improve precision for this market.

📅

Holiday Periods Shift the Distribution

January and December show the highest error rates, corresponding to peak logistics stress. Seasonal anomalies shift the feature distribution enough that models trained on average conditions struggle to generalize to these periods.

💡

Order Status as a Lifecycle Signal

PENDING and PENDING_PAYMENT orders drive the most misclassifications. These mid-lifecycle states lack the clear feature signals of COMPLETE or CLOSED orders. In contrast, CANCELED and SUSPECTED_FRAUD — despite tiny counts — produce zero systematic errors due to concentrated, distinct feature profiles.

⚖️

Interpretability Doesn't Cost Accuracy

EBM matched or marginally exceeded XGBoost test accuracy (99.3% vs 99.1%) while delivering full inherent interpretability and the most balanced FN:FP ratio. The traditional performance–transparency trade-off does not hold here.

🔍

EBM Enables Direct Operational Decisions

Unlike XGBoost's SHAP post-hoc layer, EBM's feature contribution curves are part of the model itself. A supply chain manager can read why a specific order was flagged — and act immediately without a data science intermediary.

Error Overlap: Where Models Agree and Disagree

434 errors unique to LR — eliminated by nonlinear models, exposing LR's linear boundary limitations.
77 errors shared by all three models — "hard cases" with extreme time pressure, sparse features, or ambiguous order states.
123 errors shared by XGBoost + EBM (54% of EBM's total errors) — the difficulty is in the data itself, not the model architecture.

Profile of Hard-to-Predict Orders

T-tests across misclassified samples identified features significantly different from the full dataset distribution (p < 0.05). Hard orders share a consistent operational profile:

Same-day shipment Processing time ≈ 0 days Low profit margin Pacific Asia destination January / December orders PENDING or PENDING_PAYMENT status "Other" product category (binned) Extreme scheduled lead time values
